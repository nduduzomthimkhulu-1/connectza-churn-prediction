CustomerID: This is just a unique identifier for each customer. It’s useful for tracking, but it won’t help with prediction, so we won’t include it in modelling. Churn: This is the main variable we’re trying to predict. A value of 1 means the customer left (churned), and 0 means they stayed. It’s a binary outcome. UsageFrequency: This tells us how often a customer uses the service each week. I think lower usage could be a sign that the customer isn’t very engaged, which might increase their chances of leaving. SupportTickets: This is the number of support queries raised in the last 3 months. A higher number here might suggest the customer is having problems, which could make them more likely to churn. Age: The age of the customer. This might influence how comfortable they are with tech or how likely they are to explore other providers. SubscriptionTier: This shows whether the customer is on a Basic, Standard, or Premium plan. I expect Premium users might be less likely to leave since they’re paying more and probably getting better service. SatisfactionScore: This is how satisfied customers said they were (on a scale of 1 to 10). It’s a direct measure of happiness with the service, so low scores here probably connect to a higher chance of churn. DataUsage_GB: This is how much data the customer uses per month. People who use more data might depend more on the service and be less likely to switch providers. ContractDuration_Months: This tells us how long their current contract is. I’d expect that customers with longer contracts are more “locked in” and therefore less likely to churn.

THE LIMITATIONS OF THE LPM The LPM can output values less than 0 or greater than 1, which are not valid probabilities. This leads to interpretation issues and can result in poor prioritization of customers at risk of churn. For example a predicted value of 1.65 isn’t interpretable, and it may incorrectly flag a customer as extremely high risk The variance of residuals is not constant in LPMs, and relationships may not be linear. This weakens the reliability of the estimated effects, making it harder for ConnectZA to make data-driven business decisions. This could lead to poor prioritization of retention resources. The company might focus too much on customers who aren’t actually at high risk while missing those who are, just because the model can’t capture complex relationships properly. Although the LPM is simple and interpretable, its mathematical limitations can reduce the accuracy and usefulness of predictions
